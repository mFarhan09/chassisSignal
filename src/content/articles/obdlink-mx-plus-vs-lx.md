OBDLink MX+ vs LX comes down to platform and vehicle-network requirements. Both officially support legislated OBD-II protocols. **MX+ supports iOS, Android and Windows and adds Ford MS-CAN and GM SW-CAN. LX supports Android and Windows, is not iOS compatible, and omits those advanced Ford and GM networks.**

OBDLink currently lists MX+ at **$139.95** and LX at **$89.95** in the US, a $50 difference before tax or shipping. Those are dated direct prices as of August 27, 2026. Choose from the required app and vehicle function, not a claim that one adapter is universally faster or more reliable.

## What all legislated OBD-II protocols does not mean

Standard OBD-II primarily covers regulated emissions-related data and trouble codes. It does not promise access to every manufacturer module or service function. OBDLink's MX+ page explicitly adds Ford MS-CAN and GM SW-CAN; LX's page tells buyers to choose MX+ when those networks are required.

Even with the correct physical network, the selected app must implement the vehicle-specific function. Confirm the intersection of adapter, phone/computer OS, app, vehicle, model year and intended module. An adapter alone does not create capabilities the app or vehicle does not expose.

## App compatibility controls the purchase

OBDLink's compatible-app directory marks support per app, platform and adapter. For example, an iOS app may list MX+ but not LX because LX does not support iOS. Android applications can support both while exposing different vehicle-specific functions.

Use this sequence:

1. Name the exact task: standard code reading, live data, a supported OEM module, coding or service work.
2. Select the app that documents that task for the exact vehicle.
3. Check the app's adapter list for MX+ and LX.
4. Check device OS support.
5. Only then compare price.

## Conditional choice

Choose **MX+** when you use iOS, need a documented Ford MS-CAN or GM SW-CAN path, or need the MX+-specific OEM/app bundle. Verify the exact function rather than assuming all manufacturer modules are available.

Choose **LX** when Android or Windows is the host, legislated OBD-II is the job, the chosen app explicitly supports LX, and advanced Ford/GM networks are not required. That is a narrower, lower-cost fit—not a statement that LX is inferior for its supported job.

## Ownership and safety

Both adapters advertise automatic sleep/wake, firmware updates and protected pairing. Use OBDLink's current firmware procedure for the exact model and platform. Do not update firmware during an unstable power or connection state.

Read [OBD app vs handheld scanner](/guides/obd-app-vs-handheld-scanner/) when phone dependence, subscriptions, screen, updates or offline use are the main buying constraints. This comparison does not instruct immobilizer bypass, key programming, odometer alteration, emissions defeat or unsupported ECU changes.

## Bottom line

MX+ earns its higher current price when iOS or documented Ford/GM proprietary-network access is required. LX is the lower-cost fit for supported Android/Windows apps and standard OBD-II work that does not need those networks. Verify the exact app, vehicle and function before buying either.
