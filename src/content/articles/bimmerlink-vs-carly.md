BimmerLink is usually the cleaner choice for a BMW or MINI owner who wants control-unit diagnostics, live values, logging, and a short list of clearly named service functions. Carly is the broader app-and-scanner ecosystem: it combines diagnostics with model-dependent coding and maintenance features, but its paid access and renewal terms deserve the same attention as its feature list.

The choice is not “simple app versus advanced app.” First name the job, then confirm the exact vehicle, function, phone platform, adapter, and current license. If coding is the main goal, compare Carly with a coding-led tool rather than assuming BimmerLink is its direct substitute. If diagnosis is the main goal, do not pay for a longer feature list until the required BMW module is confirmed.

## The fast decision

Choose **BimmerLink** when your priority is BMW-focused fault access, live sensor work, data logging, battery registration, parking-brake service mode, service reset, or another function explicitly listed for your car. Choose **Carly** when you want its wider mix of diagnostics, coding, maintenance guidance, and multi-brand options, and you accept the current paid-plan model.

Choose neither until the maker's current selector or app view confirms the exact car and named job. “BMW supported” is not enough.

## What BimmerLink is built around

BimmerLink's official feature page centers on diagnostics. It lists reading and clearing trouble codes from control units, a customizable live-value dashboard, CSV data logging, and several named actions. Those actions include battery registration, service reset, electric parking-brake service mode, and diesel particulate-filter functions on applicable vehicles. Other controls, such as exhaust-flap or Active Sound Design settings, depend on factory equipment.

That specificity is useful. It lets a buyer ask whether the desired function appears for the car instead of treating every menu item as universal. The published vehicle-family list is broad, but it is still only the first gate. Model year, engine, control unit, and adapter path can narrow what works.

BimmerLink also separates the app from the interface. A supported Bluetooth, Wi-Fi, ENET, or wired adapter is a separate part of the working chain. The correct choice depends on vehicle generation and phone platform. Our [BimmerLink cost guide](/guides/bimmerlink-pricing/) explains why the adapter can materially change the first-year total.

## What Carly adds—and what it asks you to verify

Carly spans a wider set of owner tasks. Its current support material describes full diagnostics for supported brands, live data, maintenance features, a used-car check, and BMW coding. It also distinguishes those manufacturer-specific capabilities from basic OBD access. For brands or vehicles outside the deeper coverage, the available functions may stop at generic OBD diagnostics, clearing, and parameters.

Coding is the most important difference in this comparison. Carly says available BMW coding options depend on model and year, and the control unit must support the requested option. That makes Carly relevant when configuration changes are part of the brief. It does not make every listed coding possible on every BMW.

Carly's commercial structure is another difference. Hardware, selected brand coverage, optional services, region, and renewal terms can affect the actual checkout. Treat the current quote and renewal screen as purchase evidence. The [BimmerCode vs Carly guide](/guides/bimmercode-vs-carly/) is the better comparison if coding—not diagnosis—is the primary intent.

## Capability comparison by job

| Buyer job | BimmerLink | Carly | Purchase gate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Read BMW-specific fault codes | Central documented role | Documented for supported vehicles and access level | Confirm exact vehicle and module |
| View live data | Personalized values and CSV logging documented | Live data documented; depth varies | Confirm the needed parameter, not just “live data” |
| Battery registration | Explicitly listed | Available on compatible BMWs | Separate same-spec registration from battery configuration |
| Service reset or brake service mode | Explicitly named | Maintenance functions vary by car | Confirm the precise routine |
| BMW coding | Not the central role | Model- and control-unit-dependent | Check the exact option in the current vehicle view |
| Multiple car brands | BMW, MINI, and Toyota Supra focus | Broader brand coverage | Compare the selected plan, not the brand banner |
| Dedicated phone-free operation | No | No | Choose a handheld if phone independence matters |

The table is deliberately not a feature-count contest. One confirmed function is more valuable than ten badges that do not resolve to the target vehicle.

## Adapter fit can reverse the decision

Both paths rely on more than an app name. Record the BMW model, chassis, build year, phone operating system, and the exact adapter model before buying. A generic Bluetooth OBD dongle may fit the socket while using the wrong radio method or lacking the required vehicle path.

For BimmerLink, follow the current supported-adapter matrix. For Carly, use the current Carly scanner and access terms described for the selected plan. Do not transfer compatibility from another app merely because it uses the same physical OBD port.

## Compare cost over the period you will own it

A fair comparison includes every required component:

1. app access on the intended phone platform;
2. required scanner or adapter hardware;
3. optional add-ons needed for the named job;
4. renewal cost during the planned ownership period; and
5. replacement cost if the interface does not fit a later vehicle.

As observed in the US iOS storefront on September 2, 2026, BimmerLink's full-version purchase was $39.99; compatible adapter hardware remained separate. Carly's current plan quote and renewal terms form its usable total, and Carly says the amount varies with market and selected options. Storefront, country, tax, promotion, and plan composition can change, so the comparison must retain its date and region.

## Four buyer profiles

**The fault-finding BMW owner.** Start with BimmerLink when codes, module access, live data, and logs are the recurring tasks. Confirm the exact vehicle and adapter.

**The owner who also wants coding.** Put Carly on the shortlist, then verify each desired coding option. Also compare a coding-focused app before deciding.

**The household with several brands.** Carly's broader coverage may reduce the number of apps, but only if the required manufacturer-specific functions exist for each vehicle. A multi-brand label can still conceal basic-only access.

**The owner who dislikes phones and accounts.** This comparison may be the wrong category. A BMW-aware handheld can be easier to keep in the car, though its module and service coverage still need evidence. The [battery-registration tool guide](/guides/bmw-battery-registration-scanner/) shows how that category changes the buying equation for one named job.

## Which app to choose

BimmerLink has the clearer fit for BMW-centered diagnostics, live data, logs, and its explicitly listed service tasks. Carly has the broader fit when diagnostics and supported BMW coding need to live in one ecosystem and the annual economics are acceptable.

The responsible final check is the same for both: exact BMW, exact control unit or function, exact interface, exact phone platform, and current payment terms. If one of those fields is blank, postpone the purchase rather than letting a broad compatibility statement fill it in.
