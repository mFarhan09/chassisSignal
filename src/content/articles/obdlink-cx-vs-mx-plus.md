OBDLink CX and MX+ can both suit a BMW owner. They are not interchangeable across every vehicle fleet or software plan. CX is the lower-cost Bluetooth Low Energy model OBDLink describes as optimized for BMW. MX+ carries broader protocol coverage and a wider included OEM-diagnostics proposition.

The correct choice depends on the cars, app, phone or computer, and included software—not an undocumented claim that one is universally faster or more reliable.

If BimmerLink is the app in your plan, use the [BimmerLink adapter guide](/guides/bimmerlink-adapter/) to verify the exact vehicle, platform, interface, and function intersection.

## The short answer

Choose **CX** for a supported BMW/MINI, BimmerCode-centered setup when its stated protocol limits do not affect the fleet. Choose **MX+** when one adapter must span more brands, including older Ford/GM network needs, or when included OBDLink OEM upgrades matter.

Both support Android and iOS. OBDLink lists Windows for both, but CX requires a separate BLE adapter on Windows.

## The documented differences

The current [OBDLink comparison](https://support.obdlink.com/support/solutions/articles/43000713351-which-obdlink-adapter-is-right-for-me-) controls the table below.

| Factor | CX | MX+ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Position | BMW-optimized BLE adapter | Broad multi-brand Bluetooth adapter |
| Protocols | ISO 15765-4, 14230-4 and 9141-2 | Those plus J1850 PWM/VPW |
| Extra networks | No Ford MS-CAN or GM SW-CAN | Both listed |
| Stated limitation | Not for GM, Ford and certain pre-2008 FCA vehicles | Broader all-protocol proposition |
| Mobile OS | Android and iOS | Android and iOS |
| Windows | Yes, with BLE adapter | Yes |
| BimmerCode | Supported | Supported |
| OBDLink OEM upgrades | Not the MX+ bundle | Included according to OBDLink |

This evidence does not establish real-world latency, wake behavior or connection-stability superiority.

## Protocol coverage in practical terms

The extra protocol rows matter only when they intersect the vehicles the adapter must serve. CX covers the ISO protocols OBDLink lists for its BMW-focused role. MX+ adds SAE J1850 PWM and VPW plus the Ford MS-CAN and GM SW-CAN networks identified in the official matrix. Those additions are not abstract “more compatibility”: they are the reason a buyer with older Ford or GM vehicles should not assume CX is a discounted substitute.

FCA needs the same care. OBDLink explicitly warns that CX is not intended for certain pre-2008 FCA vehicles. A household with a supported late-model BMW and an older Chrysler-family vehicle therefore has a mixed requirement even if the immediate app purchase is BimmerCode.

The practical audit is simple: list every vehicle that must work now, then add the vehicle categories likely during the adapter's ownership. If any requires a network absent from CX, the MX+ premium buys coverage that can prevent a second adapter purchase. If the fleet stays within CX's documented protocols, unused breadth has no diagnostic value by itself.

## BMW focus does not mean BMW-only

CX retains its listed ISO protocols and standard OBD-II role on compliant vehicles. “Optimized for BMW” describes intended fit. The meaningful constraint is OBDLink’s explicit exclusion of older networks and affected vehicles. An owner with an older Ford, GM or certain FCA model should not treat CX as a cheaper MX+.

## BLE, Windows and platform fit

Both adapters have Android, iOS and Windows paths, but the connection architecture is not identical. CX uses Bluetooth Low Energy. OBDLink's matrix qualifies its Windows support with a compatible BLE adapter, so a desktop or laptop buyer must verify that hardware rather than reading “Windows” as a complete setup. MX+ uses its listed Bluetooth connection path without that CX-specific requirement.

On Android or iOS, the app remains the controlling compatibility source. The phone seeing an adapter does not prove the chosen app supports that adapter, vehicle and function. BimmerCode lists both CX and MX+ and describes CX as made for BimmerCode, which makes CX a focused BMW/MINI choice. That statement does not extend automatically to every third-party app.

Windows also changes the software layer. OBDLink's app material separates mobile OBDLink software from OBDwiz on Windows. A buyer who expects the same included application on every operating system should verify the exact entitlement and feature set before treating platform support as equivalent.

## When MX+ earns its premium

MX+ makes the clearest case when broader protocols or bundled software replace another purchase. OBDLink’s [app page](https://www.obdlink.com/obd-apps/) lists its mobile app and OBDwiz, while the [FAQ](https://www.obdlink.com/faq/) says MX+ includes OBDLink OEM add-ons. Third-party apps can still cost extra.

For a BMW-only BimmerCode workflow, those entitlements may not matter. For a household or workshop spanning brands, they can be the main reason to buy MX+.

## Compatibility is a three-party promise

The vehicle, adapter and app must agree. [BimmerCode’s adapter list](https://bimmercode.app/adapters/) lists both CX and MX+ for all series in its selector and calls CX made for BimmerCode. That supports adapter compatibility; it does not prove every coding option exists on every BMW/MINI or that either adapter behaves identically in other apps.

## Ownership scenarios

### BMW or MINI plus BimmerCode

CX is the focused answer when the exact vehicle is supported, the phone or Windows BLE setup is compatible, and no other vehicle needs CX's excluded networks. MX+ also appears on BimmerCode's supported list, so it remains viable when broader fleet coverage matters more than minimizing the adapter purchase.

### Mixed-brand household

Start with the oldest and least compatible vehicle, not the newest BMW. If the household includes older Ford, GM or affected FCA vehicles, MX+ has the documented protocol advantage. The value is consolidation: one adapter may cover jobs that would otherwise require another interface.

### OBDLink app or OBDwiz buyer

Confirm which application belongs to the intended operating system and which enhanced diagnostics are included. MX+ includes the OBDLink OEM add-on proposition described by the vendor; third-party apps and their subscriptions remain separate purchases. Do not count a software entitlement twice or assume an included add-on replaces a specialized BMW app.

### App-first buyer outside BimmerCode

Use the app developer's adapter list as the first gate. Then apply OBDLink's vehicle and protocol limits. If the app lists only one model, that decides the adapter even when both devices can communicate with the vehicle at the generic OBD-II layer.

## Costs beyond the adapter

Total ownership can include a paid third-party app, platform-specific software, OEM diagnostic add-ons and a second adapter if the fleet expands. MX+ may cost more initially but avoid that second purchase; CX may be the efficient choice when broader protocols and bundled OEM coverage would remain unused. These are scenario economics, not a universal value ranking.

## Before checkout

1. List every current and likely future vehicle.
2. Check the app developer’s vehicle and adapter lists.
3. Confirm Android, iOS or Windows connection requirements.
4. Decide whether MX+ OEM add-ons replace a separate purchase.
5. Price third-party apps independently.
6. Treat performance claims as unverified without controlled testing.

## Bottom line

CX is the cleaner fit for BMW/MINI-first BimmerCode use when its protocol limits are irrelevant. MX+ is the stronger architecture for a mixed-brand fleet, older Ford/GM needs or buyers who value included OBDLink OEM diagnostics. Match the adapter to the whole vehicle-and-app plan.
