Use the BMW Condition Based Service screen to see what the car says is due. Use iDrive service history and itemized invoices to check what someone recorded as completed. If a seller points to a future oil-service date, ask to see the corresponding old entry or invoice before counting the oil change as verified. BMW's U.S. maintenance booklet describes the due-date display. Its service-history FAQ describes a separate record saved to the car by the service partner. [BMW maintenance booklet](https://www.bmwusa.com/content/dam/bmw/marketUS/common/warranty-books/2025/BMW_MY25_Maintenance_with_BEVs.pdf) · [BMW electronic service history FAQ](https://faq.bmwusa.com/s/article/Teleservice-Use-Electronic-service-history-displayed-only-in-the-vehicle-aRsP2?language=en_US)

## Compare one service item at a time

| Evidence | What to look for | What it can support |
|---|---|---|
| CBS or service requirements | The exact item and its displayed date, distance or due state | The maintenance status currently calculated or recorded by the vehicle |
| iDrive service history, if available | An entry for that item, plus the date and mileage displayed | A service was entered in the electronic history |
| Itemized garage invoice or work order | The vehicle, date, mileage if recorded, and the actual work and parts | A named provider billed or recorded the specific work |
| Inspection finding | What the technician observed and what they could access | The current condition of an inspected item |

Keep rows separate for engine oil, brake fluid and any other items on the exact car. Verify the brake-fluid change against its own line on the work order, even when the shop also changed the oil that day.

## Check the records at the car

1. Open the maintenance or service requirements screen on the car. Write down the relevant items and the date or mileage each one shows. Menu paths vary by iDrive version and model. For recent U.S. models, BMW's booklet shows examples under vehicle status or service requirements. [BMW maintenance booklet, maintenance display overview](https://www.bmwusa.com/content/dam/bmw/marketUS/common/warranty-books/2025/BMW_MY25_Maintenance_with_BEVs.pdf)
2. Open service history separately if the vehicle shows it. Ask for the entry that matches the item the seller says was done. BMW says the electronic history is viewed in iDrive and depends on the service partner saving the entry. An empty view leaves the electronic evidence incomplete. [BMW electronic service history FAQ](https://faq.bmwusa.com/s/article/Teleservice-Use-Electronic-service-history-displayed-only-in-the-vehicle-aRsP2?language=en_US)
3. Match the entry against the invoice. Check that the invoice belongs to the car and that the item, service date and recorded mileage are plausible together. Compare units if a car or invoice uses miles and the other uses kilometres.
4. Put any gap on the inspection list. Write “oil invoice missing” or “brake-fluid entry has no corresponding work order” so the buyer or technician can follow up on a specific item.

Keep the service counters intact during a records review. Ask the car's owner before photographing paperwork and redact personal details before sharing a copy.

## What common mismatches mean for the buyer

| What you see | What to check next |
|---|---|
| Future oil-service date, no oil history entry or invoice | Ask who changed the oil, when they did it, and whether they can supply the work order. Treat the date as evidence of the current reminder setting. |
| An independent garage invoice, no matching iDrive entry | Verify the vehicle and work on the invoice. Check whether the shop recorded the service electronically. |
| A history entry says service was done, and the same item is already due | Compare dates, mileage and the exact item. Ask the servicing shop or inspector to explain the mismatch. |
| The seller shows a My BMW App reminder | Ask to see the car's iDrive history and the paperwork for the claimed work. |

An invoice for oil and filter at 78,200 miles lets you record that documented service and compare it with the car's mileage now. A brake-fluid reminder belongs on its own row, with the corresponding record or an open question for the inspector.
